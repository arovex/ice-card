About this card

A small, free tool for a moment that matters. Here is why it exists, how it is built, and what it deliberately does not do.

Why this exists

Most of us carry the information a helper needs, but it sits behind a passcode. In an emergency, the people nearby may not be able to unlock your phone, and you may not be able to tell them anything.

This site is a simple answer to that gap: a name, one or two phone numbers, and the few medical facts that matter, somewhere they can actually be seen. Not a full medical history — just enough to start the right conversation.

What it does

The form takes about three minutes, and every field is optional. From what you enter, the site draws a finished image in your browser.

  • A lock-screen wallpaper, with the top and bottom left clear for the clock, flashlight and camera.
  • A wallet card the size of a credit card, with a front and a back.
  • An A4 sheet with four front-and-back pairs, for family members or a bag.

You can save a PNG, copy the image, or print the sheet. A card can also include a QR code that carries your contact details as plain text — not a link to a website.

What it never does

Nothing you type is sent anywhere. There is no backend, no account system and no analytics.

  • No sign-up, no email, no password.
  • No uploads, no cloud copy, no syncing between devices.
  • No advertising, no tracking pixels, no profiling.
  • No request for a bank PIN, card number or password — and the card reminds helpers that a bank will never ask for a PIN.
  • No pretence that this replaces official records or emergency services.

The only outside request is for the optional fonts. If they do not load, the site falls back to fonts already on your device and everything still works.

How it is made

The site is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript: no framework, no build step, no package manager and no server. The card is drawn with the Canvas API, including the QR code, so it can run from a folder on a computer with no connection at all.

Layouts are drawn at print resolution — 1170 × 2532 for a typical lock screen and 2480 × 3508 for A4 — so the text stays sharp on a phone and on paper. The typefaces are Fraunces and Inter; the icons and card artwork are drawn in the page itself.

Made to be read under stress

The card favours large type, strong contrast and short lines. Text on the lock-screen layout is at least 28 px at full size, and content that will not fit is shortened or dropped with a warning rather than spilling off the card.

The site works with a keyboard, shows a visible focus ring, labels every control, and has light and dark themes. Tap targets are at least 44 px. There is a right-to-left layout for Urdu. Animation uses transform and opacity only, and switches off when your device asks for reduced motion.

What it is not

This card is a convenience, not a medical record. It is not a medical device, and it does not call anyone for you. In an emergency, call your local emergency number first, then use the card to help the people around you.

Keep your official records up to date with your doctor, and set up your phone’s built-in Medical ID or emergency information as well. Use both if you can.

Corrections and translations

This is an independent, non-commercial project. There is nothing to buy, no newsletter and no mailing list. The site does not ask who you are.

All interface text lives in one file, js/strings.js, so a correction or a new language can be made in one place. The drawing code is in js/card.js. Everything is readable and can be edited or translated.

If a phrase is wrong, unclear, or missing in your language, it can be fixed without changing the layout.

Ready in three minutes.

Your details stay in your browser. You can change or delete them at any time.
